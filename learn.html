Welcome · 3 minutes

Bring one real idea.

This course gives you a system for the conversations where someone else needs to get on board.

By the end, you will be able to:

  • Understand the person across the table.
  • Name what you need to happen.
  • Connect the idea to what matters.
  • Listen for the concern underneath pushback.

The required path takes about an hour. Once you finish, you can come back for as many practice reps as you want.

Baseline · 7 minutes

Make the pitch before you learn the model.

This gives you a real starting point. Write what you would actually say, without trying to make it perfect.

The situation

You are a TextNow intern. Your team proposes an in-app feature that helps users recognize suspicious messages. You want a four-week pilot using one product designer and two engineers, which delays an onboarding experiment by one sprint.

Avery, the executive sponsor, wants ideas that solve a real problem and can be tested quickly. Nine of 14 users reported uncertainty about unexpected messages. Six of eight prototype participants noticed the warning and chose a safer action. The test was small and did not measure long-term impact.

Your task: Get Avery to sponsor the four-week pilot.

Target Audience · 8 minutes

You are never pitching to a room.

You are pitching to a person. Start with their world, not with everything you know.

Macro and micro

Macro is the context around them: their role, responsibilities, priorities, pressures and constraints.

Micro is what matters to this person right now: their motivations, concerns, decision style and current state.

Micro is situational awareness. It is not a personality test.

End Goal · 8 minutes

Know what you want.

“Share information” is not an end goal. The outcome of sharing it is.

Four kinds of end goals

Action: approve, commit, delegate or assign budget.

Understanding: see a tradeoff, risk or new angle.

Relational: shift trust, alignment or role clarity.

Process: create a new rhythm, owner or way of working.

Clarity · 8 minutes

Make it easy to follow.

Use structure to connect the idea to what matters. The most important piece is the “so what.”

WhatYour idea or ask.
So whatWhy it matters to this person.
Now whatThe next step and timing.
Pushback · 10 minutes

Find the concern underneath the no.

Pushback is information. Your first job is to understand it before you defend the idea.

The loop

  1. Reflect the content and the concern.
  2. Check whether you understood.
  3. Ask an open question to find the interest underneath.
  4. Ask permission before offering a path forward.
Final rep · 10 minutes

Try the pitch again.

Use what you know about Avery, your end goal and the impact that matters. Keep it light, bright and tight.
