Welcome · 3 minutes

Bring one real idea.

This course gives you a system for the conversations where someone else needs to get on board.

By the end, you will be able to:

  • Understand the person across the table.
  • Name what you need to happen.
  • Connect the idea to what matters.
  • Listen for the concern underneath pushback.

The required path takes about an hour. Once you finish, you can come back for as many practice reps as you want.

Baseline · 7 minutes

Make the pitch before you learn the model.

This gives you a real starting point. Write what you would actually say, without trying to make it perfect.

The situation

You are a TextNow intern working on an innovation challenge. TextNow is prioritizing user trust and wants to reduce harmful interactions without creating unnecessary warnings for legitimate messages.

Your team proposes an in-app feature that identifies signals associated with suspicious messages, explains why a message may be risky and offers safer next steps. You want a four-week pilot using one product designer and two engineers, which delays an onboarding experiment by one sprint.

What Avery cares about

Avery sponsors the challenge and is accountable for choosing projects that produce credible learning and can earn support from product and engineering. Avery cares about user trust, speed to evidence and making good use of limited design and engineering time.

What you know

Nine of 14 users reported uncertainty about unexpected messages. Six of eight prototype participants noticed the warning and chose a safer action. The test was small and did not measure long-term impact. The pilot would test usage, comprehension and false warnings.

The decision

Avery can sponsor the pilot, but product and engineering leaders must approve the people and sprint time. You have five minutes with Avery.

Your task: Get Avery to sponsor the four-week pilot.

Target Audience · 12 minutes

Learn from the person across the table.

You are never pitching to a room. You are pitching to a person. Audience alignment starts by asking, listening and checking what you heard.

Know what you are listening for

Macro is the world around Avery: responsibilities, priorities, pressures and constraints.

Micro is what matters to Avery in this conversation: motivations, concerns, decision style and current state.

You are learning what is true right now, not diagnosing Avery's personality.

Five active-listening tools

  1. Tell me more. Invite depth without steering.
  2. Repeat the last few words. Encourage Avery to continue.
  3. Paraphrase. Reflect what you heard.
  4. Ask open questions. Learn concerns, not just facts.
  5. Use tentatifiers. “It sounds like…” or “I might be wrong, but…”
Correction is useful.
If Avery corrects your paraphrase, you learned something you did not know.

End Goal + Pushback · 12 minutes

Know what you want. Then listen for the no.

A useful conversation moves toward an outcome. When the other person pushes back, understand the concern before defending the idea.

Four kinds of end goals

Action: approve, commit, delegate or assign resources.

Understanding: see a tradeoff, risk or new angle.

Relational: shift trust, alignment or role clarity.

Process: create a new rhythm, owner or way of working.

Make the ask light, bright and tight

Light: no extra context.

Bright: obvious what “yes” means.

Tight: stop once you have made it.

Plan a second-best outcome.
A smaller test or another conversation can still move the work forward.

When Avery pushes back, loop before you solve

  1. Reflect the content and the concern.
  2. Check whether you understood.
  3. Ask an open question to find the interest underneath.
  4. Only then ask permission to offer a path forward.

Positions sound like “we cannot spare the engineers.” The interest might be speed, credibility or the cost of delaying other work. Solve for the interest.

Clarity · 12 minutes

Make the impact easy to see.

Facts do not carry their own meaning. Structure the message, then use a story or metaphor to help Avery see why it matters.

WhatYour idea or ask.
So whatWhy it matters to Avery.
Now whatThe next step.

In a live conversation, say one part and check in before moving on. The “so what” does most of the work.

Option 1: Metaphor

Name the idea's core function in one phrase. Find something familiar that does the same job.

A useful metaphor explains impact. It does not exist to sound clever.

Option 2: Pain to Promise

Today → Every day → Imagine if → Because of that → So that → The ask

Use the spine as a guide, not a script.
