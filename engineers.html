Client-facing engineers and consultants

What is the client actually asking for?

For client-facing engineers, solutions engineers, technical consultants and forward-deployed engineers who need to understand a client's business and adapt their advice to it.

A client asks whether you can deliver sooner. Your engineer explains the dependencies. But the client might be worried about a commitment they have already made to their boss. Until your team understands that, they can keep giving a technically accurate answer that leaves the real concern untouched.

  • A client questions your recommendation, and your team needs to understand the concern before defending the solution.
  • An engineer explains how something works, but the client needs to understand what it changes for their business.
  • A scope change or technical limitation needs explaining, and the client needs a useful next step as well as an answer.
  • Your contact understands the recommendation, but still has to explain its value to the people who approve it.

Practice listening when the client pushes back.

When a client says, “That won't work for us,” there is more to learn. Participants practice saying back what they heard, checking their understanding and asking about the concern underneath it. And then they use what they learn to change the recommendation, the explanation or the next step.

The model

The TECH Model

TECH gives your team a shared way to prepare for an important conversation, notice when their answer is missing the concern, and change their approach. It starts with who they are talking to and what matters to that person.

TTarget Audience

Know who you're talking to, what matters to the business, and what matters to them.

EEnd Goal

Ask clearly for what you want, and know how you'll handle pushback.

CClarity

Use stories and metaphors to make complex work easy to grasp and its impact on your audience obvious.

HHow You Say It

Use pace, volume, tone, and pauses to make the important parts land.

What they practice

Bring the conversation you need to have.

Participants bring a recommendation they need a client to support, a technical explanation that keeps getting misunderstood, a conversation about scope or timelines, or a design doc that needs sign-off. They work out who they are speaking to, what that person needs and what they want to happen next. Then they practice the conversation, get direct feedback and try again.

Not public speaking training.

This is stakeholder management: connecting technical work to what other people care about, so it gets understood, approved and acted on.

Questions

Communication training for engineers: FAQ

Is this public speaking training?

No. It is stakeholder management training. The focus is the conversations engineers have with executives, colleagues and clients: understanding what the other person needs, explaining recommendations and responding to concerns. Participants practice delivery too, including pace, tone and pauses, so the important parts are easier to follow.

How do engineers get buy-in from executives and product teams?

Start with the decision-maker, not the technical detail. Know what matters to the business and to that person, ask clearly for what you want, make the impact easy to grasp, and know how you will handle pushback. The TECH Model turns those steps into a habit engineers practice on real proposals.

How can engineers communicate better with clients?

Start by asking what the person needs to decide and what matters to them. Say back what you think you heard and check it before recommending a solution. Then connect your explanation to that need, make the next step clear and leave room for questions. If they push back, find out what is behind the objection and let that change your response. TECH gives your team a shared structure for practicing those habits.

How do companies train forward-deployed engineers on client communication?

Give forward-deployed engineers practice on the conversations they have while working with clients: understanding the business problem, checking assumptions, explaining technical limits and responding when requirements change. In TECH sessions, participants bring real work, practice asking about the concern behind a request and adapt their recommendation to what they learn. They get direct feedback, with assessments against the same TECH rubric at the start and end of the program.

How is this different from a one-day communication workshop?

People get better at communication through practice, observation and feedback. The program runs six weekly 90-minute sessions on real work, with a baseline and endline score, a year of access to the TECH AI Coach and online course, and manager check-ins three and six months after the program.

What is the TECH Model?

TECH stands for Target Audience, End Goal, Clarity, and How You Say It. It is a four-part model for getting work understood and acted on, before and during important conversations. Michaud Garneau built it, and it is the spine of the whole program.
