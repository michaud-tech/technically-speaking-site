The TECH Model

Understand what the client needs. Make a recommendation they can act on.

For House of Common’s creative and client-facing staff. You’ll make a pitch, try the four parts of TECH, then return to the same pitch. Give yourself about 40 minutes and somewhere you can talk out loud.

T Target Audience
Understand the business need and the person making the decision.
E End Goal
Know what you want from this conversation and agree a useful next step.
C Clarity
Connect the creative work to something the client cares about.
H How You Say It
Use pace, volume, tone and pauses to help your message land.

House of Common’s published services include art direction, photography, video, campaign projects and studio rental. The right recommendation depends on the need. Sometimes that means a smaller scope, more discovery or no new project.

The client situations are fictional, not House of Common case studies or approved offers. Pitch and practice responses go to an AI coach when submitted. Use the fictional briefs rather than confidential client details. Writing and audio save on this device.

Start · 4 min

Your first client pitch

You’ve already had a first conversation with this client. Use what you know to make a recommendation before trying the tools.

A short pitch. Use the information above and your own words.

T · Target Audience · 3 min

The request and the need

A client might ask for new photos because their website feels dated. Or because buyers misunderstand the business. Or because someone senior asked for them. Those are different conversations.

Macro: What is happening in the business? Micro: What matters to this person? Listen for both before deciding what they need.

Example

“We need more social content. Our sales team says prospects still don’t understand our service.”

There may be an understanding problem here. “What are prospects getting wrong?” helps you find out before recommending more content.

“We need new team photos. Our partners keep saying the website makes us look smaller than we are, but nobody has time for another project.”Sam · Marketing manager, professional-services firm

One or two observations and one question. Treat your interpretation as something to check.

T · Target Audience · 5 min

Follow the client’s answer

Stay with what the person is saying long enough to understand it. You don’t have to prepare your recommendation while they’re still talking.

“The last shoot looked good, but we barely used the images.”Alex · Example client
Tell me more
Invite them to unpack something.Tell me more about barely using the images.
Repeat the last few words
Echo the phrase with curiosity, then leave space.Barely used them?
Paraphrase
Check what you understood in your own words.So you liked the photos, but they didn’t fit what the team needed?
Open-ended questions
Ask something they can explain.Where had you hoped to use them?

Follow the answer. Useful things to discover include who needs to understand what, what happens now, and what a better result would look like. You don’t need to turn this into a questionnaire.

“We need a brand video. People visit the site, but then our sales team has to explain what we actually do.”Dani · Marketing director, training company

Try up to three turns with Dani. Understand the need before suggesting photography, video or a campaign.

One response at a time. Ask about what they said, then listen to the reply.

E · End Goal · 3 min

Agree the next step

What do you want the client to think, feel or do after this conversation? An early call might end with a shared understanding and a scoping meeting. A later one might end with approval. Make the ask fit where you are.

Example

“Could you send the two pages buyers struggle with by Thursday? Our producer and I can use them to prepare a focused scoping call.”

If those pages aren’t ready, a short call to hear the buyer questions could still move things forward.

Your turn: Lee is planning a product launch and needs a clearer visual story. You want a 20-minute call with Lee, the launch owner and your producer to clarify audience, uses, timing and budget range. Lee can arrange it.

Make it easy to respond and see what happens next.

One alternative that still helps you understand the brief. No need to discount or commit to a shoot.

E · End Goal · 4 min

A no is information.

An objection is what someone is saying. Find out why they’re saying it before you try to overcome it.

“We don’t have the budget for a big campaign.”Morgan · Teaching example
Reflect
A big campaign feels beyond what you can commit to.
Check
Is the concern the total spend, or not knowing what you’d get from it?
Explore
What would a useful first step need to achieve?
Respond
If the priority is the recruitment page, we could scope that first. Shall we review the brief with our producer?

Use what you learn. A smaller scope may help; so may deciding their existing approach is enough. You don’t need all four moves in one turn.

“We already have someone who can take photos. Why would we need all this?”Priya · Marketing director, consulting firm

You’ve suggested scoping portraits and a short film for Priya’s recruitment page. No price has been quoted. Explore the concern in up to three exchanges.

One response at a time. Ask about what they said, then listen to the reply.

C · Clarity · 3 min

Make the recommendation clear

Help a business leader follow the recommendation: what you’re proposing, why it matters, what should happen next and when. Lead with the ask if that makes it easier to follow.

Example

What: Let’s plan a focused set of team portraits.
So what: Your proposals introduce people clients will actually work with, but half the photos are missing.
Now what: Could you confirm who needs to be included?
When: Before we scope the shoot on Tuesday.

Your turn: Jo leads sales at an architecture practice. Buyers keep asking whether the team handles projects from concept through completion. Current materials show only finished buildings. Recommend a photo story that shows the process and people, and ask Jo to nominate a project and join a scoping call next week.

Connect the proposed work to the buyer confusion. Scope, permission and price still need confirming.

C · Clarity · 2 min

Explain the creative work

A familiar comparison can make creative work easier to understand. Choose one that helps this person see the purpose, not just something that sounds clever.

Example

To a sales director who gives presentations: “A shot list is like your talk outline. It helps us capture the points the audience needs, rather than come back with lots of material and no clear story.”

Your turn: Nina runs operations and organises complex events. She asks what art direction adds before a shoot. For this exercise, think of it as agreeing what the audience should understand and planning the visual choices before production.

About two sentences. Connect the comparison to a practical benefit.

C · Clarity · 3 min

Pain to Promise

Start with a problem the client recognises. Show what could be different, then connect your recommendation to that change.

Today… → Every day… → Imagine if… → Because of that… → So that… → Ask

Example

Today, your team searches three folders whenever a proposal needs a portrait. Each time, someone has to check which photo is current. Imagine a consistent set of portraits with clear names and agreed uses. The team could find what it needs, so proposal preparation could involve less chasing. Could we confirm the people and uses before we scope it?

Your turn: A hospitality group is opening a venue. Its teams use an assortment of old images, and event buyers struggle to picture the guest experience. Recommend scoping a small set of lifestyle photos for the website and sales materials. Access, participants and budget still need confirming.

A short paragraph. Explain a plausible benefit, not guaranteed bookings.

H · How You Say It · 3 min

Same words. Different meaning.

Say each version out loud. Put the emphasis on the bold word.

  1. I didn’t say we should launch it today.
  2. I DIDN’T say we should launch it today.
  3. I didn’t SAY we should launch it today.
  4. I didn’t say WE should launch it today.
  5. I didn’t say we SHOULD launch it today.
  6. I didn’t say we should LAUNCH it today.
  7. I didn’t say we should launch IT today.
  8. I didn’t say we should launch it TODAY.

Emphasizing “it” might mean something else should launch today.

H · How You Say It · 3 min

Change the delivery

Pace
Slow down on the part you want people to catch.
Volume
Try a little louder or quieter to change the emphasis.
Tone
Say the same words with curiosity, concern or confidence.
Pauses
Leave space before or after the important part.
“Could we clarify what this needs to achieve before we scope the shoot?”

Say the sentence three ways: apologizing, with genuine curiosity, and as a calm recommendation. Try changing your pace, volume, tone and pauses.

Record all three in one take, leaving a pause between versions. Then listen back to compare them.
