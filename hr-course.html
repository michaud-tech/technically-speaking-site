Sell the impact of HR

Your HR idea can be good for people and good for the business. This course helps you communicate both.

Try an executive pitch, learn the four parts of TECH, then return to the same pitch and try it again. Give yourself about 35–40 minutes, and have somewhere you can talk out loud.

E

C

T · Target Audience · 2 min

What matters to them?

There’s what matters to the business, and there’s what matters to the person you’re talking to. Both shape what they need to hear.

Your turn: Sam leads Operations. You’re discussing a proposed manager program. Sam says:

“My managers already have too much on their plates. I can’t give them another program unless I know it will solve problems they actually face.”Sam, operations leader

Think about what could matter to Sam personally and how it could affect the team or business. You’re noticing possibilities, not pitching yet.

Feedback connects to: Macro: Business and team · Micro: The person.

T · Target Audience · 3 min

Four listening tools

Use these when you need to understand what is underneath someone’s first response.

Tell me more
Invite them to keep going on something that caught your attention.
Repeat the last few words
Echo a short phrase with curiosity, then leave space.
Paraphrase
Say what you understood in your own words so they can correct you.
Open-ended questions
Ask something they can explain rather than answer with yes or no.

Your turn: Dani leads Finance and says:

“I understand why managers need support, but every proposal seems to promise better culture. I need to understand what would actually change for the business.”Dani, finance leader

Try a listening tool and see what Dani says. You can take up to three turns, then finish for a brief note.

E · End Goal · 3 min

What are you asking for?

What do you want someone to think, feel or do after the conversation? Knowing that helps you decide what to say. Then make it easy for them to answer.

Your turn: You want ten minutes in Monday’s executive meeting to propose a manager training pilot. Lee owns the agenda.

Keep it to one useful alternative.

Feedback connects to: Clear goal · Clear ask + next step.

E · End Goal · 3 min

A no is information.

An objection is what someone is saying. Your job is to understand why they’re saying it. Then you can work out what to do next.

Reflect
Say back what you heard.
Check
Find out whether you understood.
Explore
Ask what’s behind the concern or what would help.
Respond
Use what you’ve learned to offer a way forward.

Your turn: You asked Priya, a department leader, to nominate six managers for a training pilot. Priya says:

“This quarter is already packed. I can’t pull six managers away from their teams for something that might not change anything.”

Have a short conversation with Priya. You can take up to three turns, then get notes on the whole exchange.

Feedback connects to: Clear goal · Clear ask + next step.

C · Clarity · 3 min

What / So What / Now What / When

Give the listener a way to follow you: what you’re talking about, why it matters, what should happen next and when. Use the order that makes the message easiest to follow.

Your turn: Three teams have delayed performance conversations until problems escalated to HR. You want the COO to approve a two-team pilot of a manager conversation toolkit next month.

Cover What / So What / Now What / When in the order that works. You can combine parts.

Feedback connects to: Opening frame · Key evidence tied to impact.

C · Clarity · 3 min

Make it familiar

A metaphor uses something familiar to explain something unfamiliar. Choose a comparison the person you’re talking to will recognize, and show how it helps them understand your point.

Your turn: A finance leader thinks workforce planning is simply predicting an exact headcount number. They enjoy cooking.

Use something from their world and connect it to the business benefit. Two sentences is plenty.

Feedback connects to: Opening frame · Key evidence tied to impact.

C · Clarity · 3 min

Pain to Promise

Start with a problem someone recognizes, show what could be different, and connect the change to something they care about.

Today… → Every day… → Imagine if… → Because of that… → So that… → Ask

Your turn: New managers wait until a problem becomes serious before asking HR for help. HR then spends hours untangling issues that could have been handled earlier. You want the COO to approve monthly manager office hours for one quarter.

Connect the problem, the possible improvement and your ask.

Feedback connects to: Opening frame · Key evidence tied to impact.

H · How You Say It · 4 min

Same words. Different meaning.

Say each version out loud. Put the emphasis on the bold word.

  1. I didn’t say we should launch it today.
  2. I DIDN’T say we should launch it today.
  3. I didn’t SAY we should launch it today.
  4. I didn’t say WE should launch it today.
  5. I didn’t say we SHOULD launch it today.
  6. I didn’t say we should LAUNCH it today.
  7. I didn’t say we should launch IT today.
  8. I didn’t say we should launch it TODAY.

Emphasizing “it” might mean something else should launch today.

Press Record once and read all eight versions above, one after another. Pause between versions and emphasize a different word each time. Then stop and listen back to the whole recording.

H · How You Say It · 3 min

Change the delivery

Pace
Slow down on the part you want people to catch.
Volume
Try a little louder or quieter to change the emphasis.
Tone
Say the same words with curiosity, concern or confidence.
Pauses
Leave space before or after the important part.
“I think we need another day to get this right.”

Say the sentence three ways: apologizing, as a calm recommendation, and with urgency. Try changing your pace, volume, tone and pauses.

Record all three in one take, leaving a pause between versions. Then listen back to compare them.

Want to plan your delivery? Optional notes

You can mark the sentence with / for pauses, CAPITALS for emphasis, or notes such as [slow] and [quiet]. You can also just try it aloud.
